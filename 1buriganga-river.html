About the Buriganga River

The Buriganga River is one of the important rivers associated with Dhaka and the surrounding river system. The river has long been connected with the economic, social and historical development of the capital region.

Status: Under Pressure

The status shown here is a project-level awareness classification, not an official government river-health rating.

River Name

Buriganga River

Region

Dhaka, Bangladesh

BWDB Zone

North Central Zone

Flow Type

Perennial

Historical Importance

The Buriganga has played an important role in the history and development of Dhaka. River transport, trade, markets and settlements grew around the river and its connected waterways.

The river therefore represents more than a waterway. It is part of the historical landscape and economic development of Dhaka.

Environmental Pressure

The Buriganga has experienced serious environmental pressure over time. Urban development and human activities around the river can affect water quality, river space and the wider river ecosystem.

  • Water pollution
  • Discharge of untreated or inadequately treated wastewater
  • Solid-waste pollution
  • Encroachment on river space
  • Siltation and changes to the river channel
  • Pressure from dense urban development

These are general environmental pressures associated with the river; individual claims about pollution levels should be supported by specific monitoring data or official reports.

Why the Buriganga Matters

  • It is closely connected with the Dhaka metropolitan area.
  • It has historical importance for the development of Dhaka.
  • Rivers and connected waterways provide important ecological and drainage functions.
  • A healthy river system is important for people, biodiversity and the wider environment.

Conservation Priorities

Protecting the Buriganga requires long-term river management and enforcement. Important priorities include:

  • Preventing illegal encroachment
  • Controlling untreated wastewater discharge
  • Improving solid-waste management
  • Protecting natural river channels and banks
  • Regular water-quality monitoring
  • Public awareness and community participation
  • Using verified scientific and government data for restoration planning
