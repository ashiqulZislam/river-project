01 • River information

A verified river profile

The Bangladesh Water Development Board identifies Sonai as a transboundary river flowing from India into Bangladesh. In Bangladesh, the river passes through parts of Brahmanbaria and Habiganj, including Madhabpur Upazila.

02 • Then

History & people

The Sonai River is part of the water system connecting northeastern Bangladesh with the river network of the neighbouring Indian region. The project should preserve both its environmental importance and its local history.

A transboundary river

Government river information identifies Sonai as a border river with its source area in India and its Bangladeshi course extending through Brahmanbaria and Habiganj.

Connection with Madhabpur

Madhabpur is one of the Bangladeshi upazilas specifically listed by BWDB in connection with the Sonai River.

Water and local life

Rivers in the Madhabpur region support a wider landscape of agriculture, fisheries, settlements and wetland connections. A detailed historical account should be supported with dated local records and interviews.

Recent environmental pressure

Reporting published in March 2026 described waste, pollution, encroachment and narrowing of parts of the Sonai River in Madhabpur.

Archive rule: historical photographs, newspaper scans, maps and oral-history material should be added only with a traceable source, date and location. Unverified historical claims should remain clearly labelled as research leads.

06 • Future

How the river can be protected

These are project recommendations. They are not claims that every measure is already approved or implemented.

1. Stop waste dumping

Establish waste-collection points away from the river and strengthen enforcement against dumping directly into the water or along the bank.

2. Protect the river boundary

Map vulnerable sections and document encroachment using photographs, GPS/GIS data and official land records.

3. Monitor water quality

Publish regular water-quality measurements and make the results accessible to local communities and researchers.

4. Restore scientifically

Any dredging or re-excavation should be based on hydrological surveys, sediment studies and verified channel requirements.

5. Protect fisheries

Record fish species, fishing activity and biodiversity changes so restoration can consider both the river ecosystem and local livelihoods.

6. Build a public river record

Maintain a transparent timeline containing photographs, water-quality data, pollution incidents, government actions and restoration progress.
