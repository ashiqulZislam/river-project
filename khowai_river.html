01 • River information

A verified river profile

Khowai is a transboundary river associated with India's Tripura state and Bangladesh's Habiganj region. Bangladesh Water Development Board records Khowai as a perennial border river and provides hydrological monitoring stations on the river.

Perennial BWDB classification: perennial flow
Border BWDB identifies Khowai as a border river
NE Zone Bangladesh Water Development Board zone
2 Stations BWDB water-level records at Habiganj and Shaistaganj
Government information

Origin & course

The Khowai originates in the hill region of Tripura, India, before entering Bangladesh through Habiganj District. The Habiganj Sadar government information page describes the river as flowing through Chunarughat, Habiganj Sadar and Baniachong areas.

Local government record

Importance to Habiganj

The Habiganj Sadar government website describes the Khowai as historically important for communication and currently important for agriculture, fisheries and movement of goods in parts of the area.

02 • Then

History & people

The Khowai is more than a water channel. It has been connected with transportation, fishing, agriculture and everyday life in Habiganj.

A major communication route

Local government information describes the Khowai as historically serving as a major communication route for the area. Limited transportation of goods by river continues in some places.

Agriculture & fisheries

The river remains connected with agricultural water use and fisheries in the Habiganj region.

Urban pressure

As Habiganj town developed, sections of the river and its banks came under increasing pressure from waste dumping and encroachment.

Growing conservation concern

River-conservation groups and local reporting have increasingly documented concerns about the condition of the Khowai and Old Khowai.

03 • Now

Present condition

Recent reporting shows several different pressures on the Khowai system. These include waste dumping, encroachment, sand extraction, erosion and flood-related damage.

Pollution

Waste dumping

Reporting from Habiganj has documented household, commercial and other waste being dumped into or beside sections of the river.

Encroachment

Pressure on river land

Encroachment along sections of the Khowai and Old Khowai has been identified as a major conservation concern.

Erosion

Unstable riverbanks

Riverbank erosion and damage to protective embankments have created serious risks for nearby agricultural land and settlements.

Sand extraction

Pressure from extraction

In 2026, local officials and residents linked alleged illegal sand extraction with weakening of an embankment section before a breach.

Important distinction: The Khowai is not literally a completely "dead" river. BWDB currently classifies it as a perennial river. The phrase "river under severe pressure" is more accurate for this project because the evidence shows substantial environmental and physical problems while the river continues to flow.
04 • Why

Causes & consequences

1. Waste dumping

Household and commercial waste has been reported along parts of the river and its banks, affecting water, drainage and the appearance of the river.

2. Encroachment

Encroachment reduces the effective river space and can interfere with natural drainage and flood management.

3. Sand extraction

Recent reporting has raised concerns about sand extraction and its relationship with erosion and embankment stability.

4. Flood & flash-flood pressure

Heavy rainfall and upstream runoff can rapidly increase water levels, placing strong pressure on embankments and nearby communities.

5. Damaged embankments

Repeated breaches can allow river water to enter agricultural land, settlements and roads.

6. Loss of river depth and navigation

Local reporting has described reduced depth and declining navigability in parts of the river system.

05 • 2026

Recent Khowai events

July 2026 — embankment breach

Heavy rain and upstream runoff contributed to a major Khowai embankment breach in Habiganj. Reporting indicated that multiple villages were affected by flooding.

July 2026 — sand extraction investigation

Authorities seized dredgers following allegations that illegal sand extraction had contributed to weakening of the embankment.

August 2026 — another breach

A section of the Khowai protection embankment breached again after increased river flow and erosion.

August 2026 — long-term measures discussed

The State Minister for Water Resources said long-term options such as barrages or switchgates were being considered, while immediate protection included geo-bags.

06 • Government action

What authorities are doing

BWDB

River monitoring

BWDB operates water-level monitoring stations on the Khowai, including stations at Habiganj and Shaistaganj. The published hydrological dataset provides historical water-level records.

2026

Emergency embankment repair

Following the 2026 breaches, authorities initiated repair and protection work on damaged sections of the embankment.

2026

Geo-bag protection

Officials announced geo-bags as an immediate measure for vulnerable sections of the riverbank and embankment.

Long-term planning

Sustainable flood control

Government officials have discussed feasibility studies and longer-term engineering options to reduce erosion and flood risk.

07 • Future

How the Khowai can be protected

These are project recommendations for research and public discussion. They are not claims that every measure has already been approved or implemented.

1. Stop waste entering the river

Establish reliable municipal waste collection and enforce restrictions against dumping waste into the river and along its banks.

2. Protect the river boundary

Clearly identify the legal river boundary and protect it from new encroachment.

3. Control illegal sand extraction

Monitor extraction locations and enforce existing environmental and land-management rules.

4. Restore damaged banks

Use engineering studies, riverbank surveys and appropriate erosion-control methods rather than relying on temporary repairs alone.

5. Protect the Old Khowai

Map the remaining Old Khowai channel, prevent further filling and encroachment, and investigate opportunities for ecological restoration.

6. Build a public river database

Record photographs, water levels, pollution reports, maps, government projects and restoration progress with dates and source information.
